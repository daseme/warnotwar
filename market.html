Shock: how big is the oil move?

Brent spot, percent change from a year earlier. Strategists put the trouble zone at +75% to +100%. Past that, oil shocks have tended to tip economies over. This line measures the size of the shock. The rest of the page asks how anyone is taking it.

Brent spot, year over year trouble zone, +75% to +100% Feb 28 2026, start of the war

The trigger: BNO, level and jumps

The heuristic's own series. The level says which zone we are in. The jumps say when something happened. Oil reacts to strike headlines first, so a one-day move of 4% or more marks the event days better than a line the price can drift across.

Fear: is the worry priced only in oil?

OVX is how big a swing in oil the options market expects over the next month. VIX is the same for the S&P 500. The ratio is the line to watch. A high ratio means the fear is trapped in oil while stocks shrug. If the ratio falls because VIX rises, the fear is spreading.

Spread: is anyone outside the oil pit reacting?

The 20-day correlation between daily moves in stocks (SPY) and oil (BNO). Below zero is a supply shock: stocks fall on the days oil rises. Near zero or above is a shrug. This is the cleanest line for the question the site asks.

Sources and method

seriessourcenote
Brent spotEIA, Europe Brent FOB, dailyPublished weekly, so it lags the rest by up to a week. Year over year uses the last observation on or before the same date a year earlier.
BNOYahoo Finance daily closeThe heuristic's ticker. Holds front-month Brent futures and rolls them, so its return includes roll yield. That is why year over year uses spot Brent, not BNO.
OVX, VIXCboeOVX is built on WTI options, not Brent. It tracks the same fear.
SPYYahoo Finance daily closeCorrelation is Pearson on the last 20 daily percent returns, both series present.
10-year yieldTreasury constant maturityVia FRED when available, otherwise the Cboe 10-year index (^TNX), which can differ by a few basis points.
The year-over-year line has two quirks. A year ago Brent was already well off its lows, so the same price today reads as a smaller shock. And from late February 2027 the comparison rolls through the 2026 spike, so the line will drop even if nothing changes. Data refreshes on weekday evenings. Nothing is forward-filled. A gap in a line is a gap in the data.
