SALT

It is 1977. You have four salt domes, a few old brine caverns, 7.5 million barrels of Saudi light and a Congress that just watched the embargo. Run the reserve for sixty years. Every shock that really happened is coming, and a few that have not yet.

Blind: five years somewhere in the record, as the reserve really stood. Nothing on the desk says when. Name the year at the end.

push the throttle when oil is cheap · build pumps and dig caverns before you need them · repair the wells
when the strait shuts, decide how fast to pump and how much to keep
1977 · before you begin

What you control

The oil throttle. Push right to buy crude at this year’s price; pull left to sell for cash. Cheap years are the time to push.
The repairs switch. Off, routine or full. The salt slowly crushes well casings; repairs keep wells from failing and reopen shut caverns first.
The four site cards. Each dome is its own place. On its card you order pumps (two years, $350 million; until then its oil is stuck), dig caverns (three years, $40 million each, 10 million barrels of room) and fix anything shut. You start with fifteen old brine caverns, about 180 million barrels of room, and no pumps anywhere.
Hold course. Turns the year with this plan, then keeps doing it, year after year, until a shock, a decision, full caverns, empty money or a shut cavern nobody is fixing stops it. Pumps and caverns are ordered once; the throttle and the switch repeat. You get one receipt for the whole stretch.
how the money works
Congress pays. Each year you get a grant. It follows the real pattern: generous after the embargo, near zero in the nineties, a trickle after 2009, more in the refill years. A happy Congress gives up to a fifth more; an angry one, a fifth less.
Leftover money carries over, at a cost. You keep it, but Congress cuts next year’s grant by half of what you are sitting on. Spend it unless oil is dear this year and you expect it cheaper next year.
You can sell oil for cash. Pull the throttle left. Congress has allowed this for real, to pay for repairs in 2015 and for buybacks in 2023. The money is yours the same year. The barrels are gone until you buy them back.
Emergency sales pay the Treasury, not you. In a crisis you release oil for the country, and the receipts go to the general fund. Congress remembers that you helped.

When a shock comes, the year stops and you run the pumps week by week with the knob.
