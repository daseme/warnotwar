How the Claims Record Is Built

A record of reported U.S. government statements about oil and ship movement through the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf bypass routes — what was said, what each number measures, and what we can and cannot promise about completeness.

Purpose

The record retains what was claimed, puts what each number measures into a standard form, and preserves corrections, disputes and revisions. It currently holds 32 statements from March 10 through August 21, 2026, and feeds the Reading the Hormuz Numbers tools and the verification panel.

The standard form

Every recorded statement answers seven questions:

  • What? oil, oil and products, tankers, all ships
  • Where? Hormuz only, the Persian Gulf, the region including bypass routes, bypass routes only
  • Direction? outbound, inbound and outbound, unspecified
  • When? one night, one day, a 7-day average, a running total, current
  • Who? a named official, an anonymous official, an institutional statement
  • Status? on the record, revised, deleted, disputed
  • Source? exactly where it came from, with a dated link on every row

One public statement can produce several rows: a single post that gives a Hormuz rate, a bypass rate, a regional total and a one-day volume is recorded as four claims, one figure per row, each with its own scope and time basis. This is what prevents the apples-and-oranges confusion the numbers otherwise invite.

The rules the pipeline enforces

These constraints are checked mechanically every time the record is loaded; a row that violates them fails the build rather than reaching the page.

Never overwrite history. A deleted post, a same-day denial, a revised figure — all remain as separate rows, joined by links. Nothing is edited away.

Every speaker gets the same verb. Every description uses "Said." Verbs that grade credibility — claimed, confirmed, admitted, insisted — are rejected by the loader, for every speaker equally.

Contradictions sit side by side. When a different speaker contradicts a statement, the two rows are linked both ways and neither replaces the other — a denial does not "win" in the data model. A figure is only replaced when the same speaker restates the same quantity.

Each reading describes its own week. Two seven-day averages stated ten days apart describe different weeks; a lower later reading is recorded as a series member, never as a walk-back.

One figure per row; no figure for qualitative statements. A denial is not "0 vessels"; a statement with two numbers becomes two rows.

Every machine value has a plain-English display string, and a value without one fails the build instead of leaking jargon to readers.

The comparable series

Most recorded numbers cannot be directly compared with each other or with independent tracker estimates — they measure different waterways, different units, or different time windows. The pipeline builds a strict comparable_hormuz_flow_claims list: outbound, Hormuz-only, oil flow-rate claims in million barrels per day. That series (currently three claims) is what the verification gap is computed from. What is left out is shown, and left out by rule, never by judgment.

How statements are collected

Absolute completeness cannot be guaranteed: statements happen in press gaggles, television interviews, deleted social posts, anonymous briefings and unindexed remarks. The approach is to make coverage systematic and checkable instead.

tiersourceswhat it catches
1 · direct White House briefings and transcripts; Department of Energy / Secretary Wright; CENTCOM and Department of Defense releases; presidential Truth Social; official X accounts the statements themselves, verbatim context
2 · wires Reuters, AP gaggles, TV remarks, deleted posts, anonymous officials, corrections
3 · specialist Axios, WSJ, Bloomberg, S&P Global, USNI, E&E/Politico, Lloyd's List and similar operational claims and anonymous briefings

sweep keywords: Hormuz · Persian Gulf · oil · tanker · barrels · ships · Project Freedom · escort · flow · transit

The strongest completeness statement this project makes is: "all located U.S. oil-flow statements through the stated date, across these source sets." Where a month shows no entries, the timeline says "no statements located," never "no statements" — absence of located statements is not evidence of silence, and coverage varies by month.

What comes next

  • collection_runs — recording which source families were swept and through what date, so "complete through a date" has a checkable meaning;
  • verification_snapshots — attaching the independent evidence that existed at the time of each claim (tracker estimates, activity indices), without ever altering the historical claim itself;
  • later resolution — official export data and destination import statistics arriving months later are the referee that can move a claim's status from unverified to consistent or not corroborated, always with a citation.
