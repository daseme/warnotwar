What is this
about?

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway between Iran and Oman — one of the world's most important oil corridors. When officials, journalists, and analysts talk about "oil flows through Hormuz," they often use numbers that sound comparable but actually measure different things. This tool helps you read those numbers critically.

How to read
this record

These are reported statements, written down in a standard form by this project — the descriptions are records, not verbatim quotes; each links to its source. Status tags are lifecycle states (on the record, deleted, disputed), never verdicts about truth. “Anonymous” means the reporter knows who spoke; the public does not.

Nothing is overwritten: corrections and disputes are added as linked entries. Not everything is here: this is every statement located through Aug 26 across the sources swept (official channels, wire services, specialist press) — statements can happen in gaggles, deleted posts and unindexed remarks.

A–Z Glossary

mb/d
million barrels per day — a rate of flow.
consensus
the figure the tracking firms roughly agree on, sitting inside their wider range of individual answers.
AIS
the radio transponder system ships use to broadcast their position; "dark" ships have switched it off.
VLCC
the largest common class of tanker, carrying about 2 million barrels.
condensate
ultra-light oil produced with natural gas; counted in some totals and not others.
ship-to-ship transfer
moving cargo between tankers at sea, which can hide where oil came from.
bypass routes
pipelines that let Saudi (to Yanbu, Red Sea) and UAE (to Fujairah) oil leave without crossing the strait.
